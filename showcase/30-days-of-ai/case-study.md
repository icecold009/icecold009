# 30 Days of AI — Learning Case Study

- **Type:** Personal learning project
- **Duration:** 30 days
- **Focus:** Prompting, AI-assisted research, data workflows, automation, agents, and rapid prototyping
- **Audience:** Anyone interested in how I approached a month of practical AI learning

## Project overview

I completed a 30-day AI course designed around practical use rather than theory alone. Each day introduced a technique, tool, framework, or small build. The month began with prompt design and customisation, moved through data and workflow automation, and ended with AI-assisted agents, prototypes, and reflection.

The most useful outcome was not a single finished app. It was a change in how I think about AI: from treating it as a chatbot that produces text to treating it as a flexible collaborator inside a well-designed process.

## Why I did it

AI tools are changing quickly, but knowing their names is not the same as knowing how to use them well. I wanted a structured way to practise the full loop:

1. Define a useful goal.
2. Give AI enough context and constraints.
3. Inspect and refine the output.
4. Connect the result to a repeatable workflow.
5. Reflect on what should remain under human control.

The 30-day format gave me enough repetition to notice patterns, while the variety of tasks showed where different tools and approaches fit.

## The learning arc

### 1. Foundations: days 1–7

The first week focused on the interaction between a person and a model. I explored custom personalities, NotebookLM, branches in chat, temperature, Google AI Studio, custom GPTs, and the CRISPR and BUILD methods. I also practised the basics of prompting properly.

The main lesson was simple but important: the quality of an AI response depends heavily on the quality of the brief. A good prompt makes the task, context, boundaries, and expected format visible.

### 2. Information and lightweight building: days 8–15

The second phase made the outputs more concrete. I built an app using GPT Canvas, turned a CSV file into an interactive dashboard, converted CSV data into a report, and applied the DIG framework to larger amounts of information. The final days of this phase were recaps.

This connected prompting with analysis. Instead of asking for a generic summary, I could ask AI to transform the same source material into different useful forms: an interface for exploration, a report for communication, or a structured analysis for decision-making.

### 3. Automation and agents: days 16–24

The third phase moved from single outputs to workflows. I explored simple task automation, email automation using Slack, AI agents in n8n, automations for common tasks, Claude Code, website generation from a CV using Lovable, a personal productivity app, and the LOOP virtual framework.

This was the point where AI started to feel less like a writing assistant and more like part of a system. The important question became: what triggers the process, what should the AI do, what information should it receive, and where should a person review the result?

### 4. Prototyping and reflection: days 25–30

The final phase focused on making prototypes with AI and Lovable, applying the 80/20 rule to prototype building, recapping the month, documenting the experience, and reflecting on the result.

The 80/20 idea was especially practical: a prototype does not need every feature to be useful. It needs to test the riskiest or most important assumption quickly enough that I can learn from it.

## What changed in my approach

Before the course, it was easy to think of AI use as a sequence of isolated prompts. After 30 days, I had a more structured mental model:

- **Start with the job to be done.** Choose the outcome before choosing the tool.
- **Make the brief explicit.** Include context, constraints, examples, and the shape of the desired answer.
- **Use the right surface.** A notebook, chat branch, custom GPT, canvas, automation platform, or code agent each supports a different kind of work.
- **Keep a human checkpoint.** Automation should make work easier without making important decisions invisible.
- **Prototype before polishing.** A small working test can reveal more than a long planning exercise.
- **Treat recaps as part of learning.** Repetition is what turns a method into something usable.

## Tools and frameworks encountered

**Tools:** NotebookLM, Google AI Studio, GPT Canvas, Slack, n8n, Claude Code, and Lovable.

**Methods and frameworks:** CRISPR, BUILD, DIG, the LOOP virtual framework, and the 80/20 rule for prototyping.

These tools and names are part of my learning record. This page does not claim that every tool was mastered, that every prototype was production-ready, or that every automation was deployed.

## Honest reflection

The course was useful, but not every day had the same level of novelty. Days 13–15, 20, and 27 were recap days; I recorded them as recap rather than presenting them as new discoveries. Some of the tools and frameworks were introductions that I still need to practise in deeper projects.

The strongest result was cumulative. Prompting, data transformation, automation, agent design, and prototyping began to look like connected parts of one workflow. I also became more aware of the limits of AI-generated output: a fast first version still needs direction, verification, and human judgment.

## What I want to explore next

- Build one small end-to-end project that combines a clear prompt, structured data, an automation, and a human review step.
- Revisit the DIG, BUILD, and LOOP frameworks on a real personal workflow.
- Compare an AI-generated prototype with a manually built version to understand the trade-offs.
- Document the decisions, failures, and revisions—not only the final result.
- Keep testing where AI saves meaningful time and where it only creates more output to review.

## Final takeaway

Thirty days was long enough to build momentum and short enough to keep experimenting. I finished with more than a list of AI tools: I have a better process for deciding when to use them, how to guide them, and where to keep people in the loop.
