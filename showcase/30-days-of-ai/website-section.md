# 30 Days of AI

## From better prompts to practical prototypes

I spent 30 days learning how to work with AI more intentionally: first by improving the way I communicate with models, then by turning information into useful outputs, and finally by building automations, agents, and prototypes.

This was not just a tour of tools. It was a practical experiment in moving from **asking AI for answers** to **designing repeatable workflows with AI**.

### The journey

| Days | Focus | What I explored |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 01–07 | Foundations | Custom personalities, NotebookLM, chat branches, temperature, Google AI Studio, custom GPTs, the CRISPR and BUILD methods, and better prompting. |
| 08–15 | Working with information | Building with GPT Canvas, turning CSV files into dashboards and reports, applying the DIG framework, and consolidating the learning through recap days. |
| 16–24 | Automation and agents | Automating everyday tasks, sending emails through Slack, creating n8n agents, designing common AI automations, using Claude Code, building with Lovable, and exploring the LOOP virtual framework. |
| 25–30 | Prototyping and reflection | Rapid prototypes with AI and Lovable, the 80/20 rule for prototyping, creating this portfolio piece, and reflecting on the full month. |

### What stayed with me

- Prompting works best when the goal, context, constraints, and desired output are clear.
- AI becomes more useful when it is connected to a workflow rather than used as a one-off answer machine.
- Data becomes easier to understand when it can be explored visually and explained in plain language.
- Prototypes should test the important idea quickly before time is spent polishing everything around it.
- Recap days are valuable: repetition made the methods easier to recognise and apply.

### The honest version

Some days introduced a new tool or framework. Others were recap days, and I did not learn something substantially new on each of them. That is part of the record. The value of the month was cumulative: the individual techniques started to connect into a way of thinking about AI-assisted work.

### Takeaway

The biggest shift was from “What can this AI tool do?” to “What useful process can I design with it?” I finished the 30 days with a broader working vocabulary for prompting, data exploration, automation, agents, and rapid prototyping—and a clearer sense of where human judgment still matters.
